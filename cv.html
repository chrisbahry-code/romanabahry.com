Curriculum vitae

The complete record, drawn from her York University faculty profile and her own curriculum vitae.

Present position

Professor Emerita, Department of Languages, Literatures and Linguistics, York University, Toronto.

Degrees

  • Ph.D., Slavic Languages and Literatures (Russian and Ukrainian), University of Toronto, 1978. Dissertation in comparative literature (English, Russian, Ukrainian): “Taras Bulba and The Black Council: Adherence to and Divergence from Sir Walter Scott’s Historical Novel Pattern,” 454 pages. A study of the Russian historical novel in the nineteenth century, with emphasis on the historical fiction of N. Gogol and P. Kulish and the influence of Sir Walter Scott. Comprehensive examination in Russian and Ukrainian literature, April 1972. Final oral examination, September 1978.
  • Examining committee: N. C. Field, Chairman (University of Toronto); C. H. Bedford (Slavics, University of Toronto); K. Feuer, External Examiner (University of Virginia); G. Luckyj, Supervisor (Slavics, University of Toronto); P. F. Morgan (English, University of Toronto); D. Struk (Slavics, University of Toronto); G. Zekulin (Slavics, University of Toronto).
  • M.A., Slavic Languages and Literatures (Russian and Ukrainian), University of Toronto, 1969. Thesis subject: P. Kulish, Letters from a Country Homestead.
  • B.A. (Honours), French and Russian, University of Toronto, 1968.

Research interests

  • Comparative Slavic (Russian, Ukrainian, Polish) and English literature
  • East Central European film and culture
  • European studies
  • Austrian Galicia
  • Languages, literatures and linguistics
  • Genealogy and family history in Galicia
  • History of science: the early history of probiotics

Current research projects

  • Nineteenth-century Austrian Galicia: multicultural aspects of its literature and culture
  • Comparative English and Slavic literature and culture (Polish, Ukrainian, Russian) in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries
  • Subversive cinema: existentialism and Judeo-Christian themes in the films of East Central Europe and the former USSR

Languages

  • Command: English (native), Ukrainian, Polish and Russian (excellent), French (good).
  • Reading knowledge: Czech, Slovak, Slovenian, Serbian, Croatian, Bulgarian, Old Church Slavonic, German, Latin.
