Romana M. Bahry

Professor Emerita, York University

For forty-seven years Romana M. Bahry taught Ukrainian, Russian and comparative literature at York University in Toronto. She has written on Gogol, Kulish and Sir Walter Scott, on the theatre of Les Kurbas and the films of Dovzhenko, and she brought the documentary cinema of glasnost-era Ukraine to audiences across North America. Her most recent book restores her grandfather, the chemist Dr. W. S. Kindraczuk, to the history of probiotics.

Professor, writer, researcher, historian, genealogist

  • Professor Forty-seven years at York University, from 1972 to 2019. Twenty courses, from elementary Ukrainian to Tolstoy, Dostoevsky and the cinema of East Central Europe. Teaching
  • Writer Three books: a study of Walter Scott’s path into the fiction of Gogol and Kulish, a volume on glasnost in Soviet Ukraine, and the biography of a forgotten scientist. Books
  • Researcher Seven book chapters and fifteen articles, in English and Ukrainian, published in Canada, the United States, Ukraine, Poland and Australia. Publications
  • Historian She documented the glasnost years in Ukraine as they unfolded and recovered a forgotten discovery in the history of science. Her current work is the history of Austrian Galicia. Research
  • Genealogist Fifteen talks for genealogical societies in Toronto and Ottawa since 2007, on tracing families through the archives of Ukraine, Poland and Austria. Talks

Dr. W. S. Kindraczuk

Forgotten Chemist of Łańcut and Pioneer of Probiotics

A biography. York University Press, 2018.

A lost discovery. A forgotten scientist. In 1912 Dr. W. S. Kindraczuk described a new bacterium in the fermented milk of the Carpathian Hutsuls. A century later his granddaughter wrote his biography.

About the book

Glasnost and film

1989 to 1992

As the Soviet Union came apart, she brought the new culture of Ukraine to York University and carried it across North America.

Between 1989 and 1992 she organized four international symposia at York University, lectured on the new Ukrainian cinema from Seattle to New Orleans, advised the Smithsonian and the CBC, and produced eighteen documentary videos.
