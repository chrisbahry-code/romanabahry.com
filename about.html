About

Romana M. Bahry is Professor Emerita at York University in Toronto and a specialist in comparative Slavic and English literature and in East Central European film and culture. She is the author of The Path of Sir Walter Scott to Ukraine (1993) and of a biography of the probiotics pioneer Dr. W. S. Kindraczuk (2018), and the editor of Echoes of Glasnost in Soviet Ukraine (1990).

Biography

Romana M. Bahry was born in Salzburg, Austria, to parents who were refugees of the Second World War. She came to Canada as an infant and grew up in Hamilton, Ontario, where her father’s uncle had settled in the 1920s.

She studied at the University of Toronto: an Honours B.A. in French and Russian in 1968, an M.A. in Slavic Languages and Literatures in 1969, and a Ph.D. in 1978 under George S. N. Luckyj. Her dissertation examined how Gogol’s Taras Bulba and Panteleimon Kulish’s The Black Council follow and depart from the historical novel of Sir Walter Scott.

She began teaching Ukrainian at the University of Toronto in 1968, while still a graduate student, and joined York University in 1972. She became Assistant Professor in 1979 and Associate Professor in 1987. Over forty-seven years at York she taught twenty courses: Ukrainian language, literature and culture; the Russian novel, Chekhov, Tolstoy and Dostoevsky; and the film and culture of East Central Europe. She retired in 2019 and is Professor Emerita in the Department of Languages, Literatures and Linguistics.

Her research begins with the historical novel: Scott, Gogol and Kulish. It extends to the expressionist theatre of Les Kurbas and Mykola Kulish, the prose and drama of Volodymyr Vynnychenko, existentialism in the Ukrainian writers of the 1960s, and the films of Dovzhenko, Paradzhanov and Tarkovsky. She has also written on rock music, satire and youth culture in late Soviet and newly independent Ukraine.

Between 1989 and 1992 she worked at the meeting point of scholarship and events. She co-organized the symposium Glasnost in Soviet Ukraine at York University and edited its proceedings as Echoes of Glasnost in Soviet Ukraine (1990). She organized three further international symposia, was a visiting scholar at the Institute of Literature of the Academy of Sciences in Kyiv, advised the Smithsonian Institution, the CBC and the National Film Board on the new Ukrainian cinema, and produced eighteen educational documentary videos.

She served on the executive of the Canadian Association of Slavists in twelve of the years between 1987 and 2006, and has been a Fellow of Stong College at York since 1979. She has refereed for the Canada Council’s Killam Research Fellowships, for the Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council of Canada, for the Slavic Research Centre of Hokkaido University and for Canadian Slavonic Papers. In the 1990s she advised the Library of Congress, Harvard, Yale and Columbia on Ukrainian educational and documentary film.

Since the 1970s she has also traced the life of her grandfather, Dr. Włodzimierz Sylwester Kindraczuk, a chemist and pharmacist from Łańcut who in 1912 described the bacterium Bacillus carpathicus in huslanka, the fermented milk of the Hutsul shepherds of the Carpathians. Her biography of him appeared in 2018 in a bilingual English and Polish edition and was reprinted in 2021. Her current work concerns the history and genealogy of Austrian Galicia.

She works in English, Ukrainian, Polish, Russian and French, and reads nine further languages, among them German, Latin and Old Church Slavonic.
